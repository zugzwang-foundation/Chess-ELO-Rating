## Verdict

**In three sentences.** Since March 2024 the published ratings of steadily active adults have stopped falling in standard (+2 to +3 points a year) and rapid (+4 to +5), after falling 2 to 6 a year in standard from 2016 to February 2024; blitz is still slightly negative (-2, -1, -1). What still slides is the top of the list: players rated 2200 or more lose 5 to 8 points a year, largely the transfer that table 8.1.2's over-prediction of favourites implies, and active 2600+ players fell from 225 in 2015 to 150. The median of the active list also keeps falling, 11 to 16 points a year, but only because entrants arrive below the players who stop (continuing players gain), and that cross-sectional median is what Ghita's −26 and −16 a year measure.

**What is sliding, where, since when, by how much.**

1. **The cross-section of the active list.** The median rating of players with a rated standard game in the last 12 months fell 25 to 53 points a year in the windows from 2016 to February 2024 (the pandemic window 2020–21 aside, when it rose) and 11 to 16 a year since March 2024. In all 10 windows in which it fell, the composition term (entrants rated below the players who stopped) is larger than the whole fall, while the median of the players present at both ends rose by 2 to 10 points (section 3). The statistic records who is on the list, not points lost by anyone.
2. **The top of the list.** Every band from 1600 up has a negative median in every window since 2016. The reform relieved the middle: the medians of players rated 1600–1999 and 2000–2199 were -8, -8 and -10, -10 points a year in the two windows of 2022–24 and -3, -3, -3 and -3, -3, -3 in the three since. It did not relieve the top: 2200–2399 -9, -10, then -7, -6, -6; 2400–2599 -7, -7, then -6, -5, -6; 2600+ -7.5, -8, then -8, -7, -6.5 (section 2).
3. **Why the top keeps paying.** On broadcast games, table 8.1.2's over-prediction of the favourite [E2] alone costs players rated 2400–2599 0.14 points a game and players rated 2600 or more 0.17, while the lists show them losing 0.09 and 0.13 a game in 2025–26 (section 5). Since 2024 the over-prediction's implied transfer has the lists' sign in 18 of 18 band-years. It is a transfer down the list: a problem of spread, not of level.
4. **The elite.** Active players rated 2600 or more fell from 225 (October 2015) and 208 (January 2021) to 150 (October 2026); the 100th active player's rating fell from 2654 to 2626 (section 4).
5. **Older and less active adults.** In 2025–26 players aged 46–64 and 65 or more had median changes of -9 and -14 points; adults aged 25–45 with 20 or more games gained 5 to 8 and those with fewer lost 2 to 5 (section 2). Ageing and activity sort these groups as much as the scale does; the lists cannot separate them.

**Before the reform there was a second drain.** In the 2023–24 window the bands from 1600 to 2399 lost 0.26 to 0.61 points a game on the lists, against an implied over-prediction transfer of 0.07 to 0.28 in their favour (section 5): something else drained them, consistent with the research report's channel of under-rated newcomers and juniors [R 5] [R 39]. Since 2024 the 1600–2199 bands are within 0.04 points a game of the implied transfer or above it, while the 2200–2399 band still loses 0.09 to 0.19 a game more than the transfer implies.

**What the 2024 reform changed.** On the March 2024 list, 99.85 % of standard players rated below 2000 with no game in that period moved by exactly round(0.4 × (2000 − R)), and 99.98 % of those rated 2000 or more did not move; rapid and blitz show the same (section 4). The SD of active adults' ratings fell from 342 to 236 points in one list and has stayed near 234. The anchor cohort's drift turned from negative to positive. The second drain closed below 2200 and narrowed above it, and the cross-sectional slide slowed because entrants now arrive higher (newcomers' median first rating 1272 in 2023, 1564 in 2025 [E1]).

**What remains.** The table that over-predicts favourites is unchanged, so the top bands keep paying; the number of active 2600+ players keeps falling; the median of the active list keeps sliding by composition while continuing players gain; blitz has not turned. Deflation in the sense of a falling level for steadily active adults stopped in March 2024 in standard and rapid; what continues is a transfer from the top of the list to the bottom and a shrinking elite.

**Ghita's figures.** The −26 and −16 points a year in the book are the change of the median of the active rating list, before and after March 2024 ([R 5], p. 21), measured on the monthly lists of January 2021 to December 2025 (p. 63), not on its 954,805-game sample, which serves the federation measures (p. 63). The research report attributes them to the games [R 5]. Our closest equivalent gives falls of 21.9 to 51.8 points a year before and 9.2 to 19.0 after, depending on how "active" is read: his figures lie inside both ranges. The statistic is real; it describes the changing composition of the active list (section 6).

## 1 Data and definitions

- **Lists.** FIDE's monthly standard, rapid and blitz lists, 2015-02 to 2026-10 [V 3]; the converted files of `tools/data/convert_fide_lists.py`. Ratings, games in the period, K, year of birth, federation and the inactive flag.
- **The April 2026 list event (D-0008, R11).** List membership is an input to Layer 0. Every analysis below excludes the IDs first rated on the March 2026 list and absent from the April 2026 list: 15,712 in standard, 269 in rapid and 3,955 in blitz (section 7).
- **Windows.** E1's twelve-month windows: February to February up to 2024, March to March after the reform, and October 2025 to October 2026. No window contains the March 2024 list as an interior point; the 2024-03 window starts on it.
- **Per-player change ("survivors").** R(s + 12) − R(s) for players rated on both lists of a window with at least one rated game on the 12 lists after s. Groups: all of them; without players whose K on list s is 40; adults aged 25–45 (calendar year of s minus year of birth); E1's anchor cohort (adults 25–45, not flagged inactive on either list, at least 10 games in the window and 10 in the year before); six bands of R(s); age groups; adults by games in the window. Banding on R(s) builds in regression to the mean (players who reached a band partly by luck fall back), so a variant bands on R(s − 12).
- **Cross-section.** Players rated on a list with at least one rated game on the 12 lists up to it. The change of a percentile between two lists is split in two: the composition part is the change obtained by replacing the leavers with the entrants while keeping the stayers at their starting ratings; the within part is the rest, the stayers' own movement. Percentiles are nearest-rank.
- **Year of birth.** The latest non-zero year of birth on any list. 157 IDs whose latest list prints a zero year keep their earlier year here; E1 treats them as unknown, so the anchor cohort sizes below differ from E1's by a few players while its medians are identical.
  Measured: cohort sizes differ from E1's by 0 to 5 players; medians equal in 11 of 11 windows.
- **Broadcast games.** The E2 sample (SPEC-TABLE-FIT §1): standard-chess games with a result between two players rated on the list of the time control in force at the tour's start, January 2023 to September 2026, duplicates removed. ELO-4 data cutoff: no game dated after 2026-09-30 (7 dropped) and no file after 2026-09. K is each player's K on that list; FIDE's expected score is Layer 0's (`src/layer0`). Broadcast events are stronger and more international than the rated pool [E2].

## 6 Ghita's figures: his definitions and our closest equivalents (1.4)

**How he defines and measures deflation.**

- *The median player's annual loss* ([R 5], p. 21): rating percentiles are tracked across the whole population; before the March 2024 compression the median active rated player lost 26 points a year, after it 16; the 10th percentile gained most from the reform, and the 99th and 99.9th percentiles are said to have declined faster after it than before.
- *Data* (p. 63): 60 monthly standard lists, January 2021 to December 2025. *Active* (p. 65): at least one standard-rated game in the reference period, which is not specified further. Rapid and blitz are not analysed (p. 63).
- *Rating velocity* (p. 20): the mean rating change per game by age cohort, 2021–2025, with the February-to-March 2024 change excluded.
- *Deflation Index* (p. 50, following Milvang): the favourites' expected minus actual score, per game, in per cent, on an anonymised 2025 extract of about 954,805 FIDE games (p. 63): 1.67 % in domestic and 3.83 % in cross-border games (p. 50).
- *Global recalibration* (p. 49): on about one million 2025 games the logistic scale that best fits is S = 459 (95 % interval 455 to 462), against 400.
- *The June 2025 essay* (his "FIDE Ratings Revisited", republished by the Belgian federation): the shifting distribution of the standard list, March 2024 to June 2025, with the average rating falling by about one point a month; "active" is not defined there either [R 3] [R 4].

**Every difference explained.**

1. *Cross-section, not players.* His −26 and −16 are percentile slopes of the active list: they mix the movement of players with who is on the list. Section 3 splits them: entrants arriving below the players who stop account for more than the whole fall, and continuing players gained.
2. *"Active".* The book leaves the reference period open. Reading it as the month, the last 12 months or the calendar year changes the median's slope from -21.9 to -51.8 before the reform and from -9.2 to -19.0 after it; his figures lie inside both ranges, nearer the monthly reading before and the 12-month reading after, so no single reading reproduces both.
3. *The start.* January 2021 is the pandemic trough: the active list was small and stronger than usual, so any slope that starts there is steeper than one over normal years.
4. *The top percentiles.* Under every reading, the 99th and 99.9th percentiles fall more slowly after the reform than before; we do not reproduce the book's statement that they fell faster. The count of active 2600+ players and the top-100 mean do keep falling (section 4).
5. *Velocity* agrees in sign and order with his description (p. 20): juniors gain, the 19–34 cohort is near zero, 35–49 loses a little, 50 or more loses most, and the juniors' gain halved after the reform. Per game it mixes rating error with real ageing: older players' strength falls.
6. *The Deflation Index.* Our broadcast games give a higher domestic and a lower cross-border index than his whole-FIDE extract, and the same direction: cross-border above domestic in every year. Broadcast domestic games are stronger events than the average domestic game, and our cross-border games are mostly international opens and team events.
7. *The scale.* Our least-squares S is higher than his 459 because broadcast games are stronger and draw more, which flattens the curve; E2's fitted table shows the same through its level-dependent draw term (local scale 481 at level 1700 to 856 at 2700) [E2].
8. *The source of the −26/−16.* The research report attributes them to a 954,805-game study; the book computes them from the lists (p. 21, p. 63). The games serve the federation and Deflation Index measures.

## 8 Limits

- The lists carry no games. A change of published ratings is not a change of strength: a cohort can lose points because the scale moves, because its members age, or because they were over-rated. Separating these needs Layer 1 (Phase 3).
- Banding on the starting rating carries regression to the mean; the variant banded a year earlier bounds it (section 2).
- The broadcast sample is stronger and more international than the pool [E2]; the implied drain is exact for that sample and indicative for the pool. The formal test needs FIDE's game archive.
- Ghita's game extract is not public, so his Deflation Index and scale cannot be recomputed on his games; our equivalents use different games.
- "Active" in the book is not fully specified; section 6 shows how much the readings matter.
