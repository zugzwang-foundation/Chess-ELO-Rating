# E7 — Ghita's federation residuals on broadcast games: the direction test

Status: REVIEW — evidence for the proposal (prior work, the federation problem) and annex (T8.4, rung 7), session ELO-4, Phase 5.3. Generated by `analysis/e7_cross_border_report.py` from `analysis/aggregates/E7_cross_border.json`, which `analysis/e7_cross_border_extract.py` computes from the Lichess broadcast archive (https://database.lichess.org/broadcast/, CC BY-SA 4.0, attributed; aggregates only) [V 4] and FIDE's monthly standard lists (analysed, never committed or redistributed) [V 3]. Do not edit by hand. Licence: CC BY 4.0 (`docs/LICENSE-docs.md`). Ghita's book, *The Rating Revolution* (v1.0, February 2026) [R 5], is cited by page and paraphrased, and his blog post of 17 February 2026 from the verification record [VP 1]; none of his tables is reproduced. Per-federation estimates are not printed: while rung 7 is disabled they go to the QC only (annex T8.4), so this report gives the signs of the federations the book itself names with a direction, and the distribution of the rest without names.

**In brief.** On 47,213 cross-border standard games of 2025 between rated players in the broadcast archive, the weighted, recursive federation offsets point the same way as Ghita's for 17 of the 18 federations he names with a direction that have enough games here (one-sided sign test p = 0.00007), and for 21 of 22 on 2023–2026 (173,890 games, p = 0.00001); the logistic scale 400 and his 459 give the same signs. The exception is Peru, the weakest of his fifteen qualifying federations (recursive index +18 points, interval +10 to +26; [R 5], p. 53). Broadcast games of 2025 are FIDE-rated and may be among the games of his 2025 extract, so the sample is different from his, not independent of it; on the years his extract cannot contain, 2023, 2024 and 2026, 22 of 22 agree (p = < 0.00001). Their size is not compared, because the broadcast sample over-represents strong players (section 4) and because federation magnitudes are for the QC while rung 7 is disabled.

## 1 What is tested

- **Ghita's claim.** Ratings mean different strengths in different federations: in cross-border games players of some federations, mostly Asian, score well above the Elo expectation and players of several European federations below it ([R 5], pp. 23–26). He measures it with a cross-border performance index, the mean of S − E over a federation's cross-border games (p. 24), refined by weighting near-even games more and by recursion over the opponents' federations (p. 25; Appendix A, pp. 66–67), with expectations at his calibrated scale S = 459 in Part V (pp. 49, 51).
- **The directions.** Taken from two of his tables, by sign only: the base cross-border residuals of 2025 (p. 26: five federations under-rated, six over-rated) and the fifteen federations that qualify for his repair on the recursive index (p. 53, all under-rated). Together, 16 named under-rated and 6 over-rated. Among the figures he states: Vietnam +101 and China +100 points, Switzerland and Austria −64 (p. 26).
- **The test.** For each of these federations with enough games in our sample, the sign of our offset is compared with his. Under the null hypothesis that our signs are unrelated to his, each agrees with probability ½, and the count of agreements is binomial; the p-value is one-sided.

## 4 The sample's bias

| | player-games or players | median rating | <1600 | 1600-1999 | 2000-2199 | 2200-2399 | 2400+ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| cross-border broadcast games, 2025 (player-games) | 94,426 | 2257 | 2 % | 20 % | 21 % | 27 % | 29 % |
| cross-border broadcast games, 2023–2026 (player-games) | 347,780 | 2248 | 3 % | 22 % | 20 % | 27 % | 29 % |
| active rated pool, December 2025 list (players with a rated game in 2025) | 210,200 | 1712 | 31 % | 56 % | 9 % | 3 % | 1 % |

- **Who is in it.** Broadcast events are the stronger and more international end of rated chess [E2]: the median player-game in the 2025 cross-border sample is rated 2257, against 1712 for the active pool, and 2 % of player-games are below 1600, against 31 % of active players. Cross-border games are 48 % of the 2025 broadcast games between rated players with federations, where Ghita finds more than 80 % of FIDE's games domestic ([R 5], p. 23).
- **What it does to the test.** A federation's players in broadcast events are a selected part of it, stronger than its pool (the table above). Ghita's repair targets the lower bands, where he places most of the signal (pp. 51, 53, 55); our sample has few games there. Agreement in sign on this sample therefore shows that the pattern also holds for the players who play in broadcast events; it cannot show its size in the bands where he places it, and the two samples' magnitudes are not comparable.
- **Years.** Cross-border games by year: 2023 13,072, 2024 71,412, 2025 47,213, 2026 42,193 (and 43 dated 2022); 2026 runs to the data cutoff of 2026-09-30, after which no game is read (7 later games dropped).
- **Exclusions.** Games without both players rated on the list in force: 25,265; games involving the R11 batch: 1,511.

## 5 What it means for the proposal

- The direction of Ghita's federation residuals stands on a different sample and method, including years his extract cannot contain: the federation problem is real for the broadcast population, not an artefact of his data. The proposal says so and credits him with the finding and the method.
- It does not decide rung 7 (federation offsets), which stays disabled: the evidence here is directional, the sample is biased toward the strong players, in whose bands Ghita places the least of the signal, and per-federation magnitudes would need FIDE's game archive. The monitoring of annex T8.4 (cross-federation residuals to the QC, their distribution in public) is the step this supports.
- Ghita's repair for these federations (an activity bonus and a calibrated reset, pp. 55–59) is compared with ours mechanism by mechanism in `docs/research/PRIOR-WORK_2026-10-10.md`, which the proposal's prior-work section draws on.
