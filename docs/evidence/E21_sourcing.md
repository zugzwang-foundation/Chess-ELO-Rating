## 1 What was fetched, and how

Every request went to FIDE's own sites, from one tool, `tools/data/fetch_sourcing.py`, which reuses the repository's fetcher for FIDE's calculation pages (the endpoint and headers of `tools/data/fetch_fide_calculations.py`, the parser of E17) and its politeness module (`tools/data/politeness.py`): robots.txt first on every host (none publishes one: HTTP 404 from each of the 4 hosts), at least four seconds between requests, measured from an upper bound of the last logged request in any run, every file cached once, a doubled gap on HTTP 429 or 5xx, a stop after three refusals in a row or at once on a page that looks like a block, and the project's own User-Agent. Every request is logged in `data/fetch_log/ELO-D1.tsv`. The sites' terms, and every other source with what is taken and what is left, are in `docs/research/SOURCES_v1_0.md`.

## 5 The pilot: FIDE's per-player game pages of 2025

**The sample** (`tools/data/fetch_sourcing.py`, mode plan; fixed before any page was fetched). From the October 2025 standard list's active players with a rated game in a 2025 period (a period's page is drawn at random among the player's own), 75 federations: every federation with at least 1,000 rated players on that list, plus England, Scotland and Wales; 26 or 27 pages each, spread over every level band from the floor up in proportion to the square root of the band's players, at least one in each; juniors (born 2007 or later) and newcomers (first rated 2023 or later) drawn 3 times as often as others. Frame: 168,055 players, 47 % of them juniors or newcomers, holding 618,801 pages; sample: 1,327 of 2,000 juniors or newcomers (1,002 juniors, 880 newcomers). **Pilot.** The measures below were chosen after the pilot's first pages had been seen (`docs/DECISIONS.md`, item 32).

### 5.1 What a page gives

- **Fetched and read:** 2,000 of 2,000 pages; E17's parser read 1,993; it rejects 7 pages (game rows printed without K: 7), rows that the top 100's pages never print and a larger run's parser must accept; 1,991 of the pages read hold a game.
- **Games:** 11,501 in 2,377 tournaments, 5.77 a page (median 5, nine pages in ten at most 10, largest 27).
- **Colours, opponents' federations and ratings:** of the 11,502 games FIDE counts on the pages read, 11,501 are printed with a colour mark, the opponent's federation and the opponent's rating; 1 game row is printed without a colour mark, and E17's parser leaves such a row out. 640 rows carry FIDE's mark of the 400-point rule, so the rating printed is the capped one. In 2,376 of 2,377 tournaments the rows read equal the games FIDE counts.
- **Against FIDE's list:** the games on the page equal the list's games column for the same player and period on 1,949 of 1,993 pages (11,501 games against 11,499).
- **Opponents:** identified by name, federation and rating on the list in force, as E17 does, in 8,508 of 11,501 games; in 8,109 the opponent is a frame player with a page for the same period, so a full fetch would see that game twice (§7).
- **Pace:** one page every 5.02 s as logged, 4,493 bytes a page.

### 11.7 What it means for the four problems

- **Countries priced differently.** Not measured here: the sample was drawn by level, not by federation, and the targeted countries sample was not run (item 33).
- **White moves first.** White's edge under table 8.1.2, from every game, lies above zero in every one of the 5 level bands (+0.018 to +0.037 a game); at most 25 points apart White scores 0.457 to 0.571 by level band, each on a few hundred games with an interval of ±0.05 to ±0.07, where the broadcast games give 0.530 to 0.540 below 2200 [E2]. The fitted tables' colour term (about 35 points) leaves an edge of −0.018 to +0.001 (the 2025 self-calibrating table) and −0.019 to +0.000 (the v2 table), the interval away from zero in 2 and 2 of 5 bands: where the interval lies away from zero the edge left is negative: there the colour term is too large, White's edge at club level being smaller than the tables' 35 points.
- **The top drains: the fair table.** Favourites score below table 8.1.2 at club level, −0.035 to +0.000 a game by gap from 0 to 399, the interval below zero in 6 of the 8 gap bins; on the broadcast games, mostly of strong players, the figure is −0.006 to −0.054 [E2]. So the table's over-prediction of favourites, which the proposal's fair table corrects, is not confined to strong players. The two fitted tables, fitted only on broadcast games, leave the favourite +0.007 to +0.030 (the 2025 self-calibrating table) and +0.010 to +0.041 (the v2 table) by gap, the interval above zero in 2 and 4 of 8 bins and below it in 0 and 0; above zero, the favourite scores more than the fitted table expects: there the table, fitted on stronger players' games, is too flat for club play. By the level of the game, table 8.1.2 leaves the favourite −0.020 to −0.016 below 2000 and −0.033 to −0.029 from 2000; the 2025 self-calibrating table +0.013 to +0.016 below 2000 (the interval above zero in 2 of 3 bands) and +0.001 to +0.004 from 2000; the v2 table +0.019 to +0.025 below 2000 (3 of 3) and +0.000 to +0.007 from 2000. From 2000 the fitted tables fit and table 8.1.2 does not; below 2000 the fitted tables err the other way, by about as much as table 8.1.2: the expectation that fits club play lies between the two. What a table refitted on the pool's own games would give is for FIDE's data; the self-calibrating method is consistent with self-calibration, not yet proven.
- **Fast improvers rated behind their strength.** Adults score below FIDE's expectation against juniors beyond what the table's own error at the same gap and colour explains in 3 of the 3 bands below 2000: junior-specific 1400–1599 −0.055 (−0.085 to −0.026); 1600–1799 −0.061 (−0.089 to −0.034); 1800–1999 −0.055 (−0.077 to −0.034).
