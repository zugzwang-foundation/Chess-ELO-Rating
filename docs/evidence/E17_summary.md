*Under this rule, with the same results — not anyone's true strength.*

**What we did.** We took the 100 highest-rated active players on FIDE's October 2026 list and replayed every standard game FIDE rated for them from January 2020 to October 2026 — the same games, the same results, the same K — twice: once under FIDE's rules as they stood at each date, and once under rung 2 of our proposal, an expected-score table fitted on broadcast games that prices White's edge and lets the curve change with the level.

**We checked our copy of FIDE's rules first.** Given the inputs FIDE used, it reproduces FIDE's published calculation in 35,620 of 35,621 games, and FIDE's published rating on every list from February 2020 to October 2026 for 95 of the 100 players; for the other 5 it is off by at most 1 point on some lists, where FIDE's list departs from its own calculation or FIDE corrected a rating, and 4 of them are level again by October 2026. Each case is listed in the evidence. One rule is our reading: FIDE's archive does not show the 400-point rule of March 2024 to September 2025, which we read as a plain cap (NOT VERIFIED); FIDE's own calculations agree with that reading in all 111 tournaments that can tell.

**What changes.** With the opponents outside the 100 held at their published ratings, in October 2026 rung 2 would rate these players 23.3 points higher on average than FIDE's rules do: 99 higher, 1 lower, from −1 to +45. 93 of them change places within the 100, by at most 19 places. Under FIDE's rules, 33 of them are rated 2700 or more and 1 is rated 2800 or more; under rung 2, 42 and 6, though rung 2's flatter table also spreads ratings further apart at the top, so these counts compare two scales as well as two rules.

**Why.** Most of it is the table: the table's effect is +23.7 points on average, colour's −0.4 (colour counts only where a broadcast gives it, 62 % of the games). The reason: in these players' games rung 2's table, fitted on broadcast games, expects less of the favourite than FIDE's table wherever the gap is below 400 points (by 0.013 to 0.026 of a point a game) and more of the underdog, and these players are the favourite in 77 % of their games, so they keep more points. Part of that is rung 2's own error: at levels 2300 to 2499 its table still expects too little of favourites [E16], and the replay cannot tell FIDE's error from rung 2's. The points come from their games against players outside the 100 (+24.3 per player); among themselves the exchange balances (−0.05).

**What this is not.** It is not a verdict on anyone's strength: the results are the same, only the rule that turns them into ratings differs. It is not a full replay either: the opponents outside the 100 keep their published ratings, though under rung 2 theirs would change too, and that is where the 100's points come from: each point by which those opponents would be rated higher or lower moves the average by about 2.4 points. So the figures, the counts above 2700 and 2800 most of all, rest on that approximation. The table was fitted on games from October 2023 to September 2026, so the replay is in sample from late 2023 and out of sample before. The 100 are chosen in October 2026, so players who fell out of the top earlier are not replayed. The method, the checks and every player's figures are in E17.
