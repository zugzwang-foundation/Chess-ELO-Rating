## 1 What is replayed

- **The players:** the top 100 active players of FIDE's October 2026 standard list (no inactivity flag; rank 100 at 2626); 100 of them have a rated game on the twelve lists to October 2026, E5's reading of active [E5].
- **The window:** each player's rating on the 2020-01 list, then every standard game FIDE rated for the player on the lists 2020-02 to 2026-10, read from FIDE's published per-player calculations (3,685 of 3,685 planned pages); nothing from the November 2026 period.
- **Layer 0** applies the rules in force at each date, transcribed in `docs/research/VERIFICATION_RATING-REGULATIONS-ARCHIVE.md` (`src/layer0_history/`): the plain 400-point cap to 2021, one upgrade per tournament from 2022, the plain cap from March 2024 (NOT VERIFIED), the 2650 exemption from 1 October 2025; table 8.1.2 (identical in every version); FIDE's K; one rounding per period.
- **Rung 2:** the v2 table's published entry at the game's level band, the narrowed guard (applies: True), colour term η = 36 points where the broadcast archive gives the colour and none otherwise, no 400-point rule; the same K and rounding as Layer 0, so only the expectation differs.
- **Together, month by month.** A player's own rating in a game is FIDE's Ro plus the player's rung-2 difference (the rung-2 replay minus the Layer 0 replay) on the list the tournament used; an opponent among the 100 likewise. **Approximation:** opponents outside the 100 keep their published ratings, although under rung 2 theirs would differ too.
- **The difference** is the rung-2 replay minus the Layer 0 replay, so that a correction FIDE made outside the games is not counted as the rule's; the rung-2 rating shown is the published rating plus the difference.

**FIDE's cap mark.** FIDE prints an asterisk beside an opponent's rating that the 400-point rule replaced: 1,557 of its 1,558 marks stand exactly 400 points from the player's Ro. ## 9 Limits

- **In sample in part.** The v2 table was fitted on broadcast games of October 2023 to September 2026; the replay's lists from November 2023 are in sample, those of 2020 to October 2023 out of sample.
- **Chosen at the end.** The 100 are October 2026's; players who left the top by then are not replayed, so the counts of section 5 for earlier lists describe these players only.
- **Opponents outside the 100** keep their published ratings; under rung 2 theirs would differ too, by amounts this replay does not estimate, in size or direction. The group's net gain comes from those games, since within the 100 the exchange balances (section 4), so the rung-2 figures, and the counts above 2700 and 2800 most of all, rest on this approximation: to first order, each point by which those opponents would on average be rated higher (lower) moves the mean difference by about +2.4 (−2.4) points (from +0.1 to +7.8 by player). Rerun with every opponent outside the 100 rated 10 or 5 points lower or higher under rung 2 at every date (R58), the replay's own ratings respond and the mean moves by less than that first-order figure:

*Under this rule, with the same results — not anyone's true strength.*

| the opponents outside the 100 under rung 2 | mean difference, October 2026 | range | players higher / lower | 2700+ under rung 2 | 2800+ under rung 2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| −10 points | +15.23 | −10 to +40 | 98 / 2 | 38 | 5 |
| −5 points | +19.28 | −6 to +43 | 99 / 1 | 40 | 5 |
| as published (the method) | +23.29 | −1 to +45 | 99 / 1 | 42 | 6 |
| +5 points | +27.51 | +4 to +49 | 100 / 0 | 44 | 8 |
| +10 points | +31.52 | +6 to +52 | 100 / 0 | 46 | 8 |

A replay of the whole pool, every opponent rated by rung 2 as well, needs every rated game: it belongs to the request for FIDE's tournament reports (proposal section 9, stage 2).

- **Rung 2's own error.** The difference mixes FIDE's error with rung 2's, and the replay cannot separate them.
