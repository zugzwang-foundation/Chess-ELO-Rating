**How large, and how sure, in 2026.** The year's table beats the frozen first table by 0.00038 nats a game, against 0.0477 over table 8.1.2: about 0.8 % of its gain over table 8.1.2. The month-block interval (−0.00050 to −0.00030) rests on blocks of three of the year's 9 months; treating each month as one observation gives −0.00056 to −0.00017 (Student's t), and each quarter, −0.00077 to +0.00003. The year's table is ahead in 8 of 9 months by log-loss and 9 of 9 by the Brier score.

**The chosen form, or the form kept.** Fitted in the form the first table has (form E, kappa exp(lambda l); one colour term eta; with the draw tail, gamma exp(mu l)), as SPEC-TABLE-FIT v1.2's 24 training months would have kept it (SPEC-SELFCAL §13), within the same caps, the 2026 table beats the frozen first table by −0.00011 (−0.00018 to −0.00007) by log-loss and −0.00001 (−0.00003 to +0.00000) by the Brier score; the chosen form adds −0.00027 (−0.00040 to −0.00017).

**What the test can say.** One year decides it, and the frozen table was fitted on 10 months against the year's 22, so the test cannot tell refitting from having more data: the result is consistent with self-calibration and cannot establish it.

## 5 Limits

- **Two tables, not seven.** The data allow tables for 2025, 2026 only (§1); the test of self-calibration rests on one year in which the year's table and the frozen first table differ.
- **Short windows.** The first table's window is ten months, cut at the March 2024 compression; the second's is 22. A window under 22 months makes no choice of form (SPEC-SELFCAL §5).
- **The caps** bind the second table to within annex T7.1's yearly changes of the first, whatever the data say; a parameter on a cap's bound is shown as such, beside the same form fitted without caps (§2, §4), and a quantity that depends on one is given without an interval.
- **More data, or refitting.** The frozen first table had ten months of games and the second table 22, so the three-way test cannot tell refitting from more data; it is consistent with self-calibration and cannot establish it (§3).
- **Six selection months.** The rule of choice compares candidates on six months with blocks of three, so its bootstrap interval rests on few distinct resamples; on the confirmation months, not used to choose, the chosen form scores better than the table in force (§2).
- **The broadcast population.** Broadcast events are stronger and more international than the rated pool; the tables describe that population until FIDE's tournament reports test the method (annex T8.6).
- **Standard only.** Rapid and blitz are not fitted: their earlier rules are not transcribed.
