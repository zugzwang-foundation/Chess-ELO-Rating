# E9 — The simulator: ten years of each rung against Layer 0, on known true strengths

Status: REVIEW — evidence for the proposal (§9, §10) and annex (T8.10, T9), session ELO-5, Phase 3. Generated by `analysis/e9_simulator_report.py` from `analysis/aggregates/E9_simulator.json`, which `analysis/e9_simulator_run.py` computes with `src/simulator/` under `docs/specs/SPEC-SIM_v1_0.md`. Do not edit by hand. Licence: CC BY 4.0 (`docs/LICENSE-docs.md`). The pool is synthetic: no player's data is used. Its parameters are calibrated on the evidence reports where they could be (E1, E2, E5, E7, E8, and Layer 1's history fit) and PROVISIONAL everywhere; section 9 lists the results that rest on assumptions the data could not pin down.

Runs: 5 seeds for each of 5 scenarios, each ten simulated years after a three-year burn-in under Layer 0, about 10,328 active players; every rung on the same simulated games as Layer 0 (pairings from Layer 0's list). Figures are means over the seeds, with their range in brackets. R1's threshold uses 20 further paired runs (section 8).

**In brief.**

- **The simulated Layer 0 reproduces what FIDE's lists show without being tuned to it:** the adults aged 25–45 whose true strength was 2400 or more at adoption lose 6.1 points a year on the list while their true strength changes by -1.5 (FIDE's lists since the 2024 reform: players rated 2200 or more lose 5 to 8 [E5]), and adults rated 2400 or more score 0.050 a game below today's expectation against juniors (broadcast games: 0.072 [E6]); today's newcomer rule publishes newcomers far above their true strength (section 2).
- **Error against true strength** (RMSE over active players, net of the level): Layer 0 143 points; rung 2 with the guard 140, rung 3 98, rung 4 140, rung 5 142, rung 6 148, rungs 2 to 6 together 88. The most accurate is rungs 2 to 6 together (88); of the single rungs, rung 3, seeds (98).
- **The slide at the top:** 6.1 points a year under Layer 0, 4.5 with rung 2 and its guard, 2.7 without the guard, 3.1 with rungs 2 to 6 together.
- **The level:** from the first year to the tenth Layer 0's anchor cohort drifts +21 points against true strength in the baseline and -38 under a junior wave; rung 6 holds it to -2 and -6. Rung 3 alone moves it -44: today's newcomer rule injects points that offset the junior drain, and accurate seeds remove that offset, which is why rung 3 needs rung 6 beside it.
- **The junior drain** on adults rated 2000–2399: -0.049 a game under Layer 0, -0.046 with rung 5; newcomers need a median of 21 rated games under Layer 0 and 21 under rung 3 before they stay within 50 points of their strength.
- **Farming** gains +0.245 points a game over ordinary play under Layer 0 and +0.050 with rung 2 and its guard.
- **R1's review threshold:** the noise of the twelve-month mean of the spread ratio has an SD of 0.0124; the smallest threshold with at most 5 % false alarms from noise is 0.025, which a ratchet at κ's annual cap trips after a median of 57 months; R20's PROVISIONAL 0.02 gives 25 % false alarms and trips on a ratchet after 50 months (section 8).

## 1 What is simulated

- **Pool.** 20,000 players at the start in six federations (relative sizes 20 : 10 : 5 : 2 : 1 : 0.5), juniors 35 %, true strengths around 1450 (juniors) and 1750 (adults), a tenth of FIDE's active standard pool [E5]; entries 2.2 % of the active pool a month with E1's age shares and the 2023 newcomers' median first ratings as their true strengths [E1]; exits by age, activity and experience.
- **Strength.** A latent strength moving each month by Layer 1's fitted drift by age (`analysis/OUTPUT_L1_history.md`), multiplied under 25 by a personal factor of mean 0.45 (coefficient of variation 0.5), and a random walk of 12 latent points a month (15 after 45), E8's choice for standard; outcomes from the D1 model with E2's standard parameters, so White's edge and draw rates by level are E2's [E2].
- **Games.** Activity rising with strength (about 10 rated games a year for club adults, 50–60 at 2600 [E5] [E8]); 85 % of events domestic; Swiss events (9, 7 or 5 rounds) and round-robins of 10.
- **Ledgers.** Layer 0 exactly (an integer port of SPEC-L0, tested against `src/layer0/`), and each rung alone on the same games: rung 2 with the guard of R17 and without it, rung 3, rung 4 from the activity record (E8's design) and from Layer 1's certainty, rung 5, rung 6, rung 7, and rungs 2 to 6 together. Rung 1 is Layer 0 itself, so the RECOMMENDED NOW package of R22 is rung 2 with its guard. Rungs 3 to 7 use a forward-filter proxy of Layer 1 that knows the pool's average dynamics (SPEC-SIM §5).
- **Scenarios.** Baseline; the baseline with twice the noise (Layer 1's history fit, c = 2.0); deflation with a junior wave; two isolated federations whose ratings start 100 too low and 60 too high; adversaries (section 7); and, for R1, a ratchet of κ at its annual cap.

- **Rung 6 holds the level** in every scenario: the true level of the anchor cohort moves far less from the first year to the tenth than under Layer 0. It does not meet R15's rule month by month. The controller answers d_t, Layer 1's own measure of the gap, and under Layer 0 d_t moved -35 points from the first year to the tenth while the true level moved +21: the proxy's latent level, fixed by the anchor's zero drift (annex T2.4) on a cohort re-formed every January, is not itself fixed against true strength, and its movements reach the controller. Whether R15's rule can be met depends on how well Layer 1 holds its level, which the full Layer 1 fit on FIDE's data, not this proxy, can show.
- **Rung 7 cannot reach isolated federations:** its offset is shrunk by n×/(n× + 2000), and a federation that plays 1 % of its games abroad has too few cross-border games for the shrinkage to let any adjustment through, so its offset decays at Layer 0's pace. That is the shrinkage working as designed: the evidence for an offset is the cross-border games themselves (annex T2.5).

- **Farming:** 30 strong adults play monthly events against fields 400 to 800 points below them; the advantage is their change a game net of their true change, minus that of 30 controls of the same strength who play ordinary events.
- **Sandbagging:** 30 adults rated 2000–2300 lose every game for a year, then play normally; their published change net of their true change after 12 and 24 months, against controls.
- **Collusion:** 15 pairs of a junior (K 40 today) and an adult rated 2400 or more (K 10) play two arranged games a month that the junior wins; the points both changes create together, per arranged game.
- **Inactivity:** 30 adults stop when their rating first exceeds their strength by 40 points and return two years later.
- **Farming in points:** the farmers' own change a game net of their true change is +0.106 under Layer 0, -0.047 with rung 2 and its guard and +0.002 without it; their controls lose under every ledger (the drain at the top), so the advantage is measured against ordinary play, as T9.4 defines it. The rungs that keep today's capped table and raise the elite's K (rung 4) or post adjustments (rung 6) let the farmer keep more of the cap's gain.

## 10 Limits

- A synthetic pool a tenth of FIDE's, in standard only, five seeds a scenario: differences of a few points between rungs are within the seeds' range.
- Pairings follow Layer 0's list for every rung (the shadow-list design), so a rung's effect on who meets whom is not simulated.
- The ledger identity of annex T6 is not evaluated line by line; the points entering, leaving through the floor and posted by adjustments are reported in the aggregates.
- SPEC-SIM §11 lists the revisions the pilot runs forced and why; none was made after these runs.
