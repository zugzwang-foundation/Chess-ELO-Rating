Generated by `analysis/e9_simulator_report.py` from `analysis/aggregates/E9_simulator.json`, which `analysis/e9_simulator_run.py` computes with `src/simulator/` under `docs/specs/SPEC-SIM_v1_0.md`. Do not edit by hand. Licence: CC BY 4.0 (`docs/LICENSE-docs.md`). The pool is synthetic: no player's data is used. Its parameters are calibrated on the evidence reports where they could be (E1, E2, E5, E7, E8, and Layer 1's history fit) and PROVISIONAL everywhere; section 9 lists the results that rest on assumptions the data could not pin down. Rerun after the v1.0 red team (`docs/review/REDTEAM_v1_0.md`): entrants' true strengths moved to today's scale, and d_t and the level chain-linked across the January re-basings of the anchor panel (SPEC-SIM §11).

Runs: 5 seeds for each of 5 scenarios, each ten simulated years after a three-year burn-in under Layer 0, with about 10,441 active rated players at adoption and 12,560 after ten years, about a twentieth of FIDE's 221,130 active rated standard players (`analysis/OUTPUT_L1_history.md`); every rung on the same simulated games as Layer 0 (pairings from Layer 0's list). Figures are means over the seeds, with their range in brackets. R1's threshold uses 20 further paired runs (section 8).

**In brief.**

- **The simulated Layer 0 reproduces what FIDE's lists show without being tuned to it, on a pool whose true outcome model is E2's fitted table, so that table 8.1.2's over-prediction of favourites is part of the simulated world:** the adults aged 25–45 whose true strength was 2400 or more at adoption lose 9.4 points a year on the list while their true strength changes by -2.3 (FIDE's lists since the 2024 reform: players rated 2200 or more lose 5 to 8 [E5]), and adults rated 2400 or more score 0.037 a game below today's expectation against juniors (broadcast games: 0.072 [E6]); today's newcomer rule publishes newcomers far above their true strength (section 2).
- **Error against true strength** (RMSE over active players, net of the level): Layer 0 106 points; rung 2 with the guard 102, rung 3 94, rung 4 103, rung 5 106, rung 6 104, rungs 2 to 6 together 92. The most accurate is rungs 2 to 6 together (92); of the single rungs, rung 3, seeds (94).
- **The slide at the top:** 9.4 points a year under Layer 0, 7.6 with rung 2 and its guard, 5.6 without the guard, 5.6 with rungs 2 to 6 together.
- **The level:** from the first year to the tenth Layer 0's anchor cohort drifts -25 points against true strength in the baseline and -104 under a junior wave; rung 6 holds it to -1 and -38. Rung 3 alone moves it -59: today's newcomer rule injects points that offset the junior drain, and accurate seeds remove that offset, which is why rung 3 needs rung 6 beside it.
- **The junior drain** on adults rated 2000–2399: -0.053 a game under Layer 0, -0.051 with rung 5; newcomers need a median of 36 rated games under Layer 0 and 36 under rung 3 before they stay within 50 points of their strength.
- **Farming** gains +0.239 points a game over ordinary play under Layer 0 and +0.034 with rung 2 and its guard.
- **R1's review threshold:** the noise of the twelve-month mean of the spread ratio has an SD of 0.0036; the smallest threshold with at most 5 % false alarms from noise is 0.01 (1 of 20 runs; exact 95 % upper bound 22 %), which a ratchet at κ's annual cap trips after a median of 30 months; R20's PROVISIONAL 0.02 gives 0 % false alarms and trips on a ratchet after 43 months (section 8).

## 1 What is simulated

- **Pool.** 20,000 players at the start in six federations (relative sizes 20 : 10 : 5 : 2 : 1 : 0.5), juniors 35 %, true strengths around 1450 (juniors) and 1750 (adults); entries 2.2 % of the active pool a month with E1's age shares, their true strengths around the 2023 newcomers' median first ratings moved to today's scale by the March 2024 compression, R + round(0.4 × (2000 − R)) [E1] [E5]; exits by age, activity and experience.
- **Strength.** A latent strength moving each month by Layer 1's fitted drift by age (`analysis/OUTPUT_L1_history.md`), multiplied under 25 by a personal factor of mean 0.45 (coefficient of variation 0.5), and a random walk of 12 latent points a month (15 after 45), E8's choice for standard; outcomes from the D1 model with E2's standard parameters, so White's edge and draw rates by level are E2's [E2].
- **Games.** Activity rising with strength (about 10 rated games a year for club adults, 50–60 at 2600 [E5] [E8]); 85 % of events domestic; Swiss events (9, 7 or 5 rounds) and round-robins of 10.
- **Ledgers.** Layer 0 exactly (an integer port of SPEC-L0, tested against `src/layer0/`), and each rung alone on the same games: rung 2 with the guard of R17 and without it, rung 3, rung 4 from the activity record (E8's design) and from Layer 1's certainty, rung 5, rung 6, rung 7, and rungs 2 to 6 together. Rung 1 is Layer 0 itself, so the RECOMMENDED NOW package of R22 is rung 2 with its guard. Rungs 3 to 7 use a forward-filter proxy of Layer 1 that knows the pool's average dynamics (SPEC-SIM §5).
- **Scenarios.** Baseline; the baseline with twice the noise (Layer 1's history fit, c = 2.0); deflation with a junior wave; two isolated federations whose ratings start 100 too low and 60 too high; adversaries (section 7); and, for R1, a ratchet of κ at its annual cap.

- **Rung 6 holds the level in the baseline** (-1 points from the first year to the tenth against Layer 0's -25) and meets R15's rule there in 91 % of months. Under Layer 0 the gap it answers, d_t, moved +26 points over the same years while the true level moved -25: with the anchor panel chain-linked, the proxy's gap follows the true level. Under a sustained junior wave the controller pays +1.36 a month on average, close to its cap of 1.5, and the level still moves -38 (Layer 0 -104); R15's rule holds in 11 % of months. The controller holds the level only while its cap exceeds the drift (annex T4.5); whether Layer 1 holds its own level as well on FIDE's data as this proxy, which knows the pool's dynamics, the full fit must show.
- **Rung 7 cannot reach isolated federations:** its offset is shrunk by n×/(n× + 2000), and a federation that plays 1 % of its games abroad has too few cross-border games for the shrinkage to let any adjustment through, so its offset decays at Layer 0's pace. That is the shrinkage working as designed: the evidence for an offset is the cross-border games themselves (annex T2.5).

- **Farming:** 30 strong adults play monthly events against fields 400 to 800 points below them; the advantage is their change a game net of their true change, minus that of 30 controls of the same strength who play ordinary events.

## 10 Limits

- A synthetic pool about a twentieth of FIDE's active list, in standard only, five seeds a scenario: differences of a few points between rungs are within the seeds' range.
- Pairings follow Layer 0's list for every rung (the shadow-list design), so a rung's effect on who meets whom is not simulated.
- The ledger identity of annex T6 is not evaluated line by line; the points entering, leaving through the floor and posted by adjustments are reported in the aggregates.
- SPEC-SIM §11 lists the revisions the pilot runs forced and the two the v1.0 red team found (entrants' scale, chain-linking), each with its reason; the runs reported here follow all of them.
