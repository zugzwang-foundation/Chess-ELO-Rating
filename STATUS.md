# Status

Status: DRAFT — where the project stands, in plain words; updated at every session close-out. Nothing here is final or published. Last updated 14 October 2026.

## Done

- **An exact, open copy of FIDE's rating rules** (Layer 0, `src/layer0/`). Its specification is ratified. It reproduces FIDE's own published calculations: every game and player of the 2025 U.S. Championship, and 56 of 58 multi-event rating periods (two are one point off, unexplained and disclosed). Earlier versions of the rules are coded beside it: 2020–2026 and 2008–2019.
- **Evidence reports E0 to E21** (`docs/evidence/`), each generated by a committed script. They cover FIDE's monthly lists since 2015 and over-the-board broadcast games since 2020. Topics include deflation, junior lag, federation residuals and the expected-score table. They also cover a simulator, today's top 100 replayed since 2020, and the leading players since 1971 on FIDE's own data.
- **The proposal, its technical annex and a short brief**, version 1.0 (`docs/proposal/`). Seven changes ("rungs") are offered, each adoptable alone except that rung 3 needs rung 6 and rung 4 needs rung 2, with a verdict for each from the evidence.
- **A comparison on the 2026 U.S. Championships, fixed before any result was read.** It was frozen in stages, each recorded in a decision record and a git tag, and the automated check fails if a frozen file changes. It includes a forecast test whose reading was fixed in advance.
- **Records**: 18 decision records, with the architect's rulings R1–R89, kept as history, and a red-team review of every working session to 11 October (`docs/decisions/`, `docs/review/`). From 12 October decisions go one dated line each in `docs/DECISIONS.md`.
- **Data terms**: the figures derived from OlimpBase's transcription of FIDE's lists carry its non-commercial terms, and README's licence section names every file that holds them. FIDE's own material is analysed locally; what is committed of it is aggregates and differences, test fixtures quoting FIDE's published calculations, three event files, short quotations of FIDE's texts and single published ratings where a table needs them (`docs/DECISIONS.md`, item 6).
- **Checks**: 705 automated tests. Every number in the documents is regenerated and compared on every pull request.
- **Light mode** (12 October): fewer process gates, the same checks. Decisions are one dated line each in `docs/DECISIONS.md`, mistakes are fixed in place with a dated line, and the proposal and brief get one review before submission (`CLAUDE.md`, "How we work").
- **Corrections** (12 October): E20 reads its check of the two tables on the greats' games by when the players were chosen, and says nowhere that FIDE's table fits those games better; Appendix F marks which rounding text was in force in 2018 as NOT VERIFIED; five statements of the decision record D-0018 are corrected in place; a test fixture's identifiers that the test does not use are placeholders.
- **The quality pass** (12 October): the proposal's summary opens with what FIDE gets at once; ruling numbers and freeze vocabulary are gone from the body; Sonas's and Regan's prior work is credited, with seven sources checked; Appendix C keeps only the sources the documents rely on, without duplicates.
- **The review** (12 October): the one review of the proposal and brief before submission, by a rating statistician, a Qualification Commission member and arbiter, and a federation official. Each of its 24 findings is fixed or answered (`docs/review/REDTEAM_v1_0.md`). Rung 2 is led by the loss today's table implies at the top on broadcast games, with the lists' own figure beside it (E5); the simulated figure is in the annex. The brief says what the steps do to juniors' own ratings and to titles, and which steps need another beside them. Section 10's reading is worded in advance, its numbers left for the results. No new freezes, and pre-registration only where the proposal promises it (`docs/DECISIONS.md`, item 11).
- **Five fixes before the championship results** (12 October): section 10's reading follows the order fixed in advance: the fixed sentence, then the small-sample statement, then the log-loss lines. The loss today's table implies for players rated 2400 or more is measured at their own levels, by level band, a split chosen after seeing the data: 0.10 points a game at 2400–2599 and 0.13 at 2600 and above, beside the 0.09 and 0.13 FIDE's lists show (E5). The Brier score stands beside log-loss in the tests on FIDE's data as a secondary measure. Appendix G is a pointer to Appendix H. Appendix F says its findings reach FIDE with the proposal. Each close-out goes to the operator's project knowledge, the full folder only when asked (`CLAUDE.md`; `docs/DECISIONS.md`, items 12 to 16).
- **E5 at every level, and who runs the pilot** (12 October): every band's figure in E5 now uses the over-prediction at the players' own levels, by level band, a split chosen after seeing the data. Players rated 2200 or more lose about what it implies: 0.18, 0.10 and 0.13 points a game at 2200–2399, 2400–2599 and 2600 and above, beside the 0.19, 0.09 and 0.13 FIDE's lists show. In 2023–24, the last year before the March 2024 reform, a second drain took 0.37 to 0.93 points a game from the bands from 1600 to 2399; since the reform it is at most 0.08. The proposal says who runs the pilot: the Zugzwang Foundation maintains the open code and runs the shadow list each month, at no cost to FIDE or to the pilot federation, which shares its rated games under a data agreement and pays nothing; FIDE or the federation can run the open code themselves at any time, and arbiters' tools do not change until FIDE adopts a step. The proposal and the brief name a contact (`docs/DECISIONS.md`, items 17 to 19).
- **The headline from 2200, who runs what, and the juniors' window** (12 October): the summary, section 2(a) and the brief open their account of the drain in two parts. Since FIDE's March 2024 reform a second drain has closed: in 2023–24 players rated 1600–2399 lost 0.37 to 0.93 points a game more than table 8.1.2's over-prediction implies, and since then at most 0.08. What remains is the top: players rated 2200 or more still lose 4 to 7 points a year on FIDE's lists, about the 5 to 8 the over-prediction implies at their own levels (E5). Rung 2 is aimed at that loss, 0.10 to 0.18 points a game. E5 speaks of timing, not cause: its comparison before and after cannot separate the reform from anything else that changed in 2024. The proposal says who runs what, in the roadmap's terms: from stage 2 the Zugzwang Foundation computes the shadow list each month for FIDE, and in stage 3 the pilot federation's domestic list too, at no cost to FIDE or the federation; either can run the open code themselves. The junior figures from broadcast games are from 2025–26, after the reform, and the proposal says so (`docs/DECISIONS.md`, items 20 to 22).
- **The deflation answer restored, the brief's link, one annex fix** (12 October): section 2(a) again answers the deflation figures in Ghita's book, a sentence the previous session had cut in error. Since the March 2024 reform the active list's median still falls, 11 to 16 points a year, but only because entrants arrive below the players who stop; steadily active adults gain 2 to 3 points a year in standard (E5). The brief again says that top players are usually favourites, so it ties the table's error to the top's loss. The annex says that E6 refitted Layer 1 on at most 36 months before each test month (`docs/DECISIONS.md`, item 23).
- **Sourcing, and a pilot of FIDE's game pages** (14 October; session ELO-D1): the greats' replay list by list, from FIDE's pages already cached, equal to E20 at every year end; three new measures on FIDE's lists, totals only: who plays the rated games, by K (in 2025, by the K on the same list, K = 40 players hold 54.6 % of the player-games, the figure in Ghita's book); juniors' climb against adults'; newcomers' first ratings against their ratings a year and two years later, each a climb, not proof of under-rating; Sonas's reports for FIDE's commission read and cited by page; a pilot of 2,000 of FIDE's per-player game pages of 2025 (every game with its colour, the opponent's rating and federation, FIDE's change reproduced by Layer 0) and a probe of 20 tournament reports (no games: an index of events with FIDE IDs). Recorded in E21 (DRAFT), with the go/no-go brief; the sources ledger `docs/research/SOURCES_v1_0.md`; the draft plan of a larger fetch, `docs/specs/SPEC-SOURCING_v1_0.md`, pending the founder's decision (`docs/DECISIONS.md`, items 24 to 32).
