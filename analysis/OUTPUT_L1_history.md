**What the strong-player bias means** (standard).

- At 2400 and above the broadcast sample covers 80 % of active rated players with a usable estimate; below 1600, 0.7 %. Juniors aged 18 or less: 2,659 of 81,759 (3.3 %).
- Rung 5 (junior compensation) can be tested only on the juniors who play broadcast events: 4,257 are eligible at the October 2026 list, most of them strong juniors whose ratings already move fast. The juniors the rung is meant for, improving players below 2000 in domestic events, are almost absent.
- Rung 3 (newcomer seeds): 2,781 unrated players appear in the fit; newcomers enter FIDE's list through domestic events that are rarely broadcast, so seeds can be tested only on the few who first appear in broadcast events.
- The tests of E6 therefore hold for the broadcast population; FIDE's tournament reports (the TRF archive) settle them for the pool.

## 6 Limits

- Broadcast games only: most of a player's rated games are missing, so σ is larger and the gates of compensation and seeds are counted on fewer games than FIDE records.
- σ is conditional on the opponents' estimates (§4.3), and the plug-in forecasts ignore it (§4.5).
- Published ratings stand in for the previous fit at the window's start (§3.4).
