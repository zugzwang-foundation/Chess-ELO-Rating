## Appendix I The greats, on FIDE's own data

Outside the body's word count. From E20 (`docs/evidence/E20_greats.md`), under SPEC-GREATS v1.0, fixed before any analysis, the rulings R74 to R79 (`docs/decisions/D-0017_rulings-on-the-greats.md`) and R80 to R84 (`docs/decisions/D-0018_rulings-on-elo-9-questions.md`) and the sessions' reviews. Its headlines are the lead over one's own era and the replay's validation and findings from FIDE's pages; the backward replay has none (R81). Every table here, as in E20, reads:

*Under this rule, with the same results — not anyone's true strength. A rating measures strength against one's own era.*

**The data, and their limits.** The study uses FIDE's own data, for analysis only and not redistributed (FIDE reserves all rights; its written permission is to be requested, and until FIDE answers nothing changes: only aggregates and differences are committed, R84), and OlimpBase's transcription of FIDE's lists under its terms, NOT VERIFIED before 2001 (checked against FIDE's own files of 2001 only) (`docs/research/DATA-PROBE_greats.md`): FIDE's lists (as OlimpBase transcribes them to 2000, FIDE's own files since 2001), FIDE's printed top lists of 2000–2019 as the Internet Archive keeps them, and FIDE's per-player rating calculations, which give every game only from the list of April 2008. **TWIC and Chessmetrics were not used**, nor ChessBase or any other game collection whose terms need permission. So the greats' lists and their lead over their own era can be shown from 1971, but their games can be replayed only from 2008, and no comparison across eras is possible.

**Who.** The 36 players ranked 1st to 3rd on any FIDE list from July 1971 to December 2019 [E20 §2], from Fischer, Korchnoi and Spassky on the first list (July 1971) to Ding, the last to join (February 2019); they stand as SPEC-GREATS fixed them, with no change after results (R82). Its rule treats ties in two ways: on the lists to April 2000, ranked by their ratings, a shared 3rd place counts, and before 1999 the ratings are multiples of five, so six players are in only that way (Polugaevsky, Mecking, Beliavsky, Vaganian, Sokolov and Salov); from July 2000 FIDE's printed order decides, so Svidler, printed 4th on the 3rd's rating in October 2006, is not among the 36.

A lead over one's contemporaries, on lists whose frequency and spread change with the era, and a maximum over a different number of windows for each player: not a ranking across eras.

**The replay, 2008–2019** [E20 §4]. Of the 36, 29 have rated games in the window. Given FIDE's own inputs, Layer 0 with the rules in force at each date reproduces FIDE's printed change in 18,490 of 18,490 games; 9 player-periods are thin and carry FIDE's change. In the greats' games FIDE applied the 350-point rule to tournaments starting up to 28 June 2009 and the 400-point rule from 9 July 2009, a date no text gives. FIDE's lists rounded a period's half point towards +∞ up to the list of July 2018 (73 of 78 half points before July 2014, every one of the 27 negative ones from July 2014), except on the list of January 2009 (3 of 3 away from zero), and away from zero from the list of November 2018 (every one of the 12 negative ones); Layer 0 rounds as FIDE's lists do, towards +∞ to October 2018 and away from zero from November 2018 (R74 and R80, readings set after FIDE's pages had been seen; the list of the change NOT VERIFIED), and the list of January 2009 keeps its periods thin.

E20 §5 also replays the same games under E18's 2025 table, **applied backwards, not fitted on that era**, with every opponent outside the 29 held at FIDE's rating (R58). It measures the distance between that table and table 8.1.2 at these players' gaps, on results 8.1.2 already fits, not anyone's strength; its figures, and the caveat on reading them, are in E20 §5 only.

**What permission would add.** With written permission from TWIC, ChessBase or Chessmetrics, the full study becomes possible: the replay could extend to the games those collections hold, across the greats' careers. FIDE's own calculations, against which a replay is checked, exist only from 2008; those collections do not record which games FIDE rated; and any comparison across eras would rest on a model's assumptions. Permission has not been sought.
